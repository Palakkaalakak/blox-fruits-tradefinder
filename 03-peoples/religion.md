# Religion

**Established: many faiths, no consensus.** No single church explains the Doom. Different continents, peoples and regions account for it differently, and none dominates.

## Why this is the right answer

The Doom scattered humanity into isolated pockets for centuries. Isolated pockets generate *their own* explanations — that is what people do in the dark. By the time the Rediscovery reconnected the world, every region had had three hundred years to become certain about something different. Religious plurality here is not a design choice; it is a **direct consequence of the Dark Age**, which is the mark of worldbuilding that holds.

## What it means in practice

**Religion is a diplomatic problem, not an institution.** This is exactly why Professor Langfield teaches Religion to future rulers as something to *use* — not to believe, not to obey, but to manage. A ruler with a hundred neighbours has a hundred sensitivities to track. Faith is a lever and a hazard.

**No church can crush a heresy.** With no dominant faith there is no inquisition, which means unorthodox thought — **Laurevinism among it** — can exist openly without being stamped out. It also means it can be *dismissed*, which is worse: not persecuted, just one more school among hundreds, easy to ignore.

**Cevine's advantage compounds.** As the cradle of the post-Doom world, Cevine's own tradition carries a prestige the others cannot claim — its faith, whatever it is, is the one that was there first. That is soft power on a scale nobody can replicate.

## The families of belief to develop

Rough taxonomy; most regions will hold a blend.

| Strand | Holds that the Doom was | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Punitive** | Judgement on the old world's pride | Recovered technology is morally suspect; a brake on progress |
| **Probative** | A test, a proving-ground | Justifies hierarchy — those who rose were meant to. The aristocracy's favourite theology |
| **Venerative** | The end of a holy age | The old Earth itself is sacred; ruins are relics, surviving books scripture, Latin and Greek sacred languages |
| **Indifferentist** | Nothing. An accident | Rare, unpopular, and the position **Laurevinism** holds via the Third Great Principle |

Note what the fourth row does: Laurevinism is not merely a philosophy in this world, it is a *religious minority position*, and an unwelcome one. It tells the grieving that no one was watching. That is a hard sell in a world built on a catastrophe, and it is why the school of thought needs a school.
