## PART 1 — WHAT YOU HAVE ALREADY BUILT (canon, locked)

### 1.1 The technological signature
This is the single most distinctive thing about your world and you seem to have arrived at it by instinct rather than decision. Evidence, all from the text:

**Pre-modern register:** war drums at a thirteenth birthday · household guards · House colours and insignias · knights, lordlings, dukes, kings, queens · "Your Graces" · a Throne (of Thalassus) · direct vassals (100+ for Karois-Marain) · embroidered shoes · marble floors as national pride · a Great Council of all rulers

**Modern/contemporary register:** glass-and-steel architecture · wristwatch · lectern and microphone · aeroplanes that can be grounded · a *hostile cyber-attack* · vaccines, waves, regional border closures · guns (800 years ago) · sevaravirus (a COVID/Black-Death analogue) · Band-Aids, a nurse's office, P.E. · calculus, economics as a taught discipline · bar with a cellar, cobblestones, streetlamps

**This is not an inconsistency — it is your premise.** A world that kept the technology and lost the political modernity. That is *exactly* what a civilisation-resetting geological catastrophe on Earth would produce: knowledge preserved, states destroyed, and the thing that rebuilt order was the family with the guards. Feudalism is not a survival of the past here; it is a *re-invention* after the disaster. Say this out loud in the book once and the whole aesthetic snaps into focus.

### 1.4 The Academy
Founded after **the murder of Queen Renilda**, by a Great Council of all the world's rulers — the first unanimous act in history. 60 students, three classes (Red, Green, Blue — deliberately non-hierarchical, as A/B/C was rejected). Five wings: three class wings, a Common Section (donut-shaped, glass, park in the hole), and the SA wing with the Great Hall and Headmistress's office. Circular plan, glass walls, ringed by light woodland — built so *"the world is constantly watching them."* Guards wear academy colours only, no House insignia. Everyone is "Your Grace" — a compromise between abolishing titles and the vanity of kings.

**Staff:** the Headmistress (unnamed, "plump/fat lady") · Herseworth (Social Relations, Head of Blue) · Tevré (Head of Red) · Langfield (Philosophy + Religion-as-statecraft) · Kategeth (literacy, rhetoric) · Ramwin (mathematics, economics, ratios of coercion) · Earnshaw (Group Six) · an unnamed P.E. instructor who teaches ambush and evasion as political instinct

The curriculum is the thesis: every subject is taught as *how to rule people*. P.E. is deception training. Religion is a tool. Philosophy is the only subject taught for its own sake — "to think on your own."

### 1.5 The emotional colour system

- green → the speaking voice, the practical, ivy and emerald eyes

### 1.6 Laurevinism already operating in the fiction (this is your spine — see below)
- **Renilda's murder is a *reditus magni*.** A Great died, and instead of furor the world chose the school. The Academy *is* the Laurevine response to death, built at planetary scale. Nobody in the book has said this yet. Someone should.
- **Robert vs. Sophie is the Fallacy of Pain, dramatised perfectly.** She blames (correct target, useless act); he refuses to be blamed and instead offers presence — *"I am speaking to you not as an enemy, as a person."* That is the Grand Conclusion, verbatim, in a teenager's mouth. And it **fails**: "You are not people." Dehumanisation is the anti-Laurevine sin, and you ended the chapter on "Robert had succeeded. Right?" — which is the honest answer. Do not resolve this early.
- **Edward is the Elpidic Equation walking.** Reality minus his *mens interna* leaves an enormous remainder; hence "the Small," hence his contempt, hence his loneliness. Meeting Opàl is a candidate *isologhetos* — he clocks it instantly ("the mind behind them was a wonder like his own") and is *frightened* by it, which is right.
- **Sophie is furor.** Quiet, cold, not explosive. She is not a villain; she is a casualty in the exact clinical sense your philosophy describes, and she needs a still-living Loved to unlock the basement door.

## PART 2 — THE QUESTIONS

Answer in any order. Answer badly and fast; you can revise. The ones marked ★ gate everything downstream.

### A. The Cataclysm ("the Sundering", TBD)
★ **A1. How long ago?** This one decision determines your entire world.
- ~300 years → survivors' grandchildren; the old world is *documented*, contested, politically live; ruins everywhere; Roman remains are museum pieces beside pre-Cataclysm skyscrapers
- ~800 years → the Elsring genocide happens *at the edge of living history*, guns are ancient, the old world is scripture and rumour
- ~5,000+ years → Earth is legend, "Rome" is a word in a dead liturgy, and your Latin/Greek Laurevine vocabulary is *sacred* language

**A2. What actually happened?** Not the mechanism — the *image*. One sentence a fisherman would use. (Supervolcano winter? Crustal displacement? Sea-level collapse exposing ocean floor as new continents? An impact?) Note: whatever it is, it must explain how *technical knowledge survived while every state died*.

**A3. What survived, physically?** Which real cities are under water, which are inland now, which mountain range is new? Which library survived and who owns it?

**A4. Is the Cataclysm over?** Aftershock world or stable world? A world that is still settling gives you weather, refugees, and dread for free.

**A5. Does anyone deny it happened?** The Fallacy of Fear at civilisational scale is *right there*, and you flagged it yourself in the philosophy ("now we ignore climate change when once barbarian invaders were ignored"). Somebody in this world is burying their head about something enormous. Who, and about what?

### B. Earth's Heritage
**B1. What is the relationship to Rome/Greece?** Your Laurevine vocabulary is Greek and Latin (isologhetos, furor, dolor, reditus magni, mens interna). Who preserved those languages, and why? A church? An academy? A single obsessive dynasty? *This institution is a major faction you haven't invented yet.*

**B2. Religion.** Langfield teaches "Religion, specifically how to *use* it as rulers" — so there is at least one organised faith, and the ruling class is cynical about it. Is it a survival of a real religion, a post-Cataclysm invention, or a cult of the world-before? What do commoners actually believe about the Cataclysm — punishment, accident, or indifference? (Note: Laurevinism's Third Great Principle says *indifferent*. So Laurevinism is a **heresy** in this world if the faith says punishment. That is a plot.)

**B3. Is Laurevinism a named thing inside the story?** Three options, pick one now:
1. It is never named — it's just how certain good characters behave. (Subtle, safest, most ASOIAF)
2. It exists as a minor school of thought Langfield teaches, and the children are the first generation to take it seriously
3. It doesn't exist yet — **one of these children invents it**, and the series is its origin story
*My read: (2) is the best of these, and it makes Langfield the most important adult in the book.*

### C. The Map
**C1.** How many sovereign states, roughly? Ten? Forty? Two hundred?
**C2.** Where is the Almani Corridor, and what does it connect? Name the two things it's a corridor *between* — that's your central conflict axis.
**C3.** The Marreni Sea is "of the South". South of what? Establish your cardinal anchor.
**C4.** Where is the Academy, and *why there*? Neutral ground? Lidia is a colonial power that committed genocide — hosting the peace school there is either deeply ironic or deeply political. Which?
**C5.** Where did the Elsring live? Their land is now someone's territory, and somebody's House was founded on it.

### D. Power
**D1. How does a knight become a duke of a sovereign state in twenty years?** (Edward's parents did.) Is nobility purchasable, electable, granted by a Great Council, or won? Whatever the answer, it is why the old-money "Small" despise Edward — he is *proof the system is arbitrary*.
**D2.** Does the Great Council still exist after founding the school, or was it one-off? A standing council changes everything.
**D3.** Who killed Queen Renilda, and does anyone know? (You do not need to answer publicly. But you need to know, because the answer is your series' engine: the peace school was built on a murder, and the murderer's child may be sitting in Class Red.)
**D4.** Where does the money come from? Ramwin teaches ratios of soldiers-to-civilians and loyal-vs-competent officials. What is the economy — post-Cataclysm salvage? Agriculture? Something only one region has?

### E. Gaia (your actual blocker)
**E1.** What does Gaia *want* that the world is denying her? Edward wants a mind to meet. Robert wants Caroline. Gaia currently wants to win a game. Not enough.
**E2.** What is House Bialmonte and where does it rank? She's placed among a Crown Prince, twins with heraldry, and a genocide's heir. Her position in that hierarchy is her character.
**E3.** She reflects mid-flight — reviews her own mistakes as "paper cuts", tactically, instantly. That is a *rare* mind, and it is not the same as Edward's or Robert's. Name what she is good at, and then name what that skill costs her.
**E4.** Rita and Raya "have a shared brain." They are her isologhetoi, or a false version of them — she's the third wheel of a two-person unit. Is that a comfort or a wound?
**E5.** She's now been moved into Class Red — with Robert *and* Sophie. Your plot just handed you the collision. What does she see that Robert can't?

## PART 3 — TOOLS

**Map — Azgaar's Fantasy Map Generator** (free, open source, browser, no install: azgaar.github.io/Fantasy-Map-Generator). Generates coastlines, then lets you layer states, provinces, cultures, religions, burgs, routes, military, and a name-generator you can train on your own phonology (Yttáry/Avrandri/Ouspré). Exports SVG/PNG/GeoJSON. This is the right tool for you — it's political-map-first, which is what JR needs.

**The Cataclysm's plausibility — GPlates** (free, open source, gplates.org). Real plate-tectonics software used by geologists. Overkill, but if you want the disaster to be defensible you can literally deform Earth's plates and export the result as your coastline, then import to Azgaar.

**Terrain/climate realism — QGIS** (free, open source) plus the *Worldbuilding Pasta* blog series on climate — it teaches you Köppen zones, prevailing winds and rain shadows well enough to place deserts and grain belts non-arbitrarily. Matters because your economy and your wars follow the grain.

**The bible itself — Obsidian** (free) or **Kanka** (open source, self-hostable, kanka.io) . Obsidian is plain markdown files with `[[backlinks]]` and a visual graph — you'd see instantly that "Elsring" links to nothing yet. Kanka is a proper worldbuilding wiki with entity types (characters, families, locations, timelines, relations) if you want structure imposed on you.

**Timeline — Aeon Timeline** is the good one but paid; the free path is a markdown table in Obsidian with one row per year, or the open-source *Timeline* (thetimelineproj.sourceforge.net).

**Heraldry — Armoria** (free, open source, azgaar.github.io/Armoria) — by the same author as the map generator, and integrates with it. You have Houses with colours already (Avrandri purple-and-gold, the academy's red-green-blue circle). Do these when you need a break from the hard questions; they're fun and they generate ideas sideways.

**Suggested order:** answer A1 → A2 → C2/C3 → then open Azgaar and draw. Don't open the map generator first; you'll get a beautiful map of nothing.
