# Political order

**Established: roughly 100–200 sovereign states.** A crowded, fragmented world of many small realms and a handful of giants.

## Why this fits everything already written

- **House Deroi.** Sophie says "never heard of it" about a fellow student's House — and she is a crown princess. In a world of fifteen powers that is an insult; in a world of two hundred realms it is simply true, and her contempt is the more revealing for being accurate.
- **The Great Council's unanimity.** The manuscript calls the vote to found the Academy the first agreement in history. Getting *fifteen* rulers to agree is a good afternoon. Getting a hundred and fifty is a miracle — which is what the text says it was. The murder of Queen Renilda must have been seismic.
- **Edward's parents.** Knights to dukes of a sovereign state in twenty years. Only possible where sovereignty is granular enough that a small realm can be built, bought or inherited within a career.
- **"Sixty children of the world's rulers."** Not one per realm — so the Academy is *selective*. Someone decided which hundred-odd realms did not merit a seat, and those exclusions are grievances. Worth knowing who was left out.

## Layered sovereignty

The 100–200 figure works best as a layered structure rather than a flat list, because the manuscript demands it: **House Karois-Marain has "over a hundred direct vassals"** and Edward's parents are dukes *of their own sovereign state*.

So:

- **Great powers** — a handful. Cevine foremost, holding the Almani Corridor and colonies on the third continent. Lidian and Réselian powers of comparable weight.
- **Kingdoms and middling realms** — dozens. Parvaan sits here: real, sovereign, and utterly outmatched.
- **Sovereign minor states** — the long tail. Duchies, principalities, city-states, some no larger than a valley. Edward's homeland is one, and it is *new*.
- **Vassals within all of the above** — Houses with their own lands, guards and incomes, sworn to a greater crown but not sovereign. Karois-Marain's hundred sit here.

The line between "small sovereign state" and "large vassal" is thin, contested, and worth a war.

## Why the world is fragmented

Because of how it was rebuilt. The Dark Age produced power at the scale of the valley and the harbour — whatever a strong family could hold and feed. When industry and communication recovered, those thousands of petty holdings consolidated only *partly*, into hundreds rather than dozens, because there was never a force capable of finishing the job.

There was no bourgeois revolution and no nationalism to sweep the map clean. Each technological leap made the incumbents richer instead of displacing them. **The map is a fossil of the Dark Age, running modern technology.**

## Consequences

- **Diplomacy is the dominant art**, which is precisely what the Academy teaches — Social Relations, Religion-as-statecraft, Ramwin's ratios of loyalty and coercion. The curriculum is shaped by the map.
- **Cevine's paramountcy is fragile.** Dominating a hundred-odd realms requires that none of them combine. Parvaan's secession threatened the precedent far more than the territory — which is motive enough for Ouspré.
- **Alliance and marriage matter enormously**, which is why sixty heirs in one building is either the wisest thing the world has ever done or the most dangerous.
