# The lost continent

**This is Europe.** Of the world's seven landmasses, this is the one the Doom drowned outright. It does not resurface. It exists today only as a shallow plateau on the seabed.

## Origin of this fact

This started as an artifact of how the base map was generated (`maps/build_world.py`), where Europe was the landmass left out when the post-Doom coastlines were built. Rather than treat that as an accident to paper over, the story was shaped to make it true in-world: Europe really did sink, and everything that follows from that is now canon, not a retcon.

## What sinking meant, in-world

Despite being far from the Marreni impact site in the south, Europe was wiped off the map entirely — sunk, not merely devastated. This is a Doom-wide consequence, not a local one: the crust failure described in `the-doom.md` §4 didn't only raise and lower land near the crater, it took a whole landmass under.

**During the Dark Age**, nobody knows this happened. Descendants of Europeans only know their homeland is lost — cut off, unreachable, not heard from — the same total silence that covers everyone else in the early Dark Age. It's not until the Rediscovery's expeditions go looking and find open ocean where Europe should be that it becomes understood, and confirmed, that Europe is not merely lost contact but literally gone, underwater.

## The Cevinese and the Fenes

Cevine and the other northern peoples of the Africa-derived landmass — collectively the **Fenes** — carry a migration-origin myth: their ancestors crossed the water from a homeland that was disappearing behind them. Given zero institutional continuity survived the Dark Age (see `04-history/chronology.md`), this story only survives as myth, warped and evolved over centuries — but it is not false. They are descended from Europeans who fled south as Europe was sinking, well before the Doom's crust failures finished the job over the following decades and centuries.

This is also the source of Cevine's strong European character and influence: not just geographic proximity to a drowned neighbour, but direct ancestry from it.
