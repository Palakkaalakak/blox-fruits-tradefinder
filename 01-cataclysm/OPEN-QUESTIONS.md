# Open questions — the Cataclysm, the Rediscovery, the Elsring

*Round two. Answer in fragments; precision comes later. Each answer unlocks the layer beneath it.*

## What the established canon already forces

Three things are now fixed, and they constrain everything else:

**Lidia and Réselia are continents.** So "Réselian" is a continental identity — the scale of saying *European*. Opàl naming herself Réselian to Edward is therefore vague on purpose. And the Academy, sitting on Lidia, sits on one of the two conquering continents. Not neutral ground.

**"They still had guns."** The most load-bearing phrase in the world so far. It means the collapse was *uneven* — some populations kept industrial capacity while most lost it. That one fact explains the Rediscovery, the Elsring genocide, and the present day's aircraft-and-household-guards strangeness. One cause, three consequences.

**Cevine is Venice with the mass of France.** The *Throne of Thalassus* is a sea-throne, which now reads as deliberate. It also makes the Parvaan betrayal far crueller: for a thalassocracy to surrender the Port of Ouspré is France giving up Marseille. It had to look ruinous, or the Parvaani would never have believed the goodwill. Which tells us Parvaan was landlocked, and desperate.

## A. The shape of the world

1. How many continents, and what are the others called? The Elsring lived somewhere — a third landmass the other two sailed to?
2. Where does Cevine sit? A naval power with France's mass needs long coastline *and* deep hinterland. A country on Lidia, on Réselia, or straddling?
3. Is the **Almani Corridor** a sea passage rather than a land one? Cevine dominating it as a naval power points that way. What does it connect, and who else wants it?
4. Are the continents new land, drowned-and-reshaped old land, or a mix? This decides whether ploughing a field in Lidia turns up Roman coins.

## B. The Rediscovery

5. How long between the Cataclysm and the Rediscovery? That gap is the Dark Age — the centuries when nobody knew the shape of the world any more. Its length sets how much was forgotten.
6. What started it? Ships finally good enough? A famine? One person's obsession? Ages of exploration begin for a reason, usually greed.
7. Race or joint enterprise? Lidia and Réselia were in the same waters at the same time and committed the genocide together. Rivals who converged, or partners who later soured?
8. Is it *over*? Anywhere still unmapped?

## C. The uneven collapse

9. Why did Lidia and Réselia keep their guns? Sheltered geography? A surviving institution? Luck of where the factories were?
10. What was the technological floor at its worst, and how fast was the recovery? Vaccines and aircraft are needed by the present day.
11. Who has the least technology *now* — and is that region a colony?

## D. The Elsring

12. What were they actually like? Numbers, organisation, what they had.
13. How is the genocide talked about today? Denied, mythologised, taught as glory, quietly regretted? In the manuscript a student cites it casually in a classroom as a lesson in the value of knowledge, with no visible discomfort. That is chilling, and worth keeping.
14. Did any survive? Descendants, a diaspora, a language, one island?
15. Whose land is it now, and which House was founded on the spoils?
