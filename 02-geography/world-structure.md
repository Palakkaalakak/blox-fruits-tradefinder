# World structure

*Established so far. Everything here follows from decisions already made; the details are open.*

## The three continents

| | |
|---|---|
| **Lidia** | Retained firearms through the Doom by luck. A Rediscovery power and co-perpetrator of the Elsring extermination. The **Academy** stands here — meaning the world's peace school sits on one of the two conquering continents, which is either bitter irony or hard politics. Famous marble somewhere (Edward's homeland). |
| **Réselia** | The other gun-bearing continent and the other conqueror. Opàl is Réselian — a *continental* identity, the scale of saying "European", and therefore a vague answer to give a stranger. |
| **The third continent** *(unnamed)* | Homeland of the **Elsring**, exterminated ~800 years ago during the Rediscovery. Today: **a mix — some states independent, some still held as colonies.** The borders between free and held are the world's rawest wounds. |

**Source landmasses — decided.** Lidia and Réselia are two separate, whole continents. **Réselia is the "Africa" continent** — Cevine, Parvaan, the Almani Corridor and the Fenes are all here. **Lidia is the "America" continent** — the Academy and Edward's marble homeland are here. They are not two halves of a single torn continent; that idea (formerly written below) was wrong and has been removed.

## The Almani Corridor is internal to one continent, not between two

**Correction.** An earlier version of this document claimed Lidia and Réselia were once a single landmass torn in two by the Doom, with the Corridor as the rift between them. That was wrong and has been removed along with everything that followed from it (the "same rock, same ancestral population" claim, the joint-genocide reasoning built on it, and the "Corridor is the one place they touch" framing). Do not reintroduce it.

**What's actually true:** `sunder_pair` was used to split **Africa** — one single source continent — into two shores, and adjusted by hand until it read as a real strait. Both shores belong to the *same* continent — Réselia. The Almani Corridor is an internal strait within Réselia, not a gap between Lidia and Réselia. Cevine and Parvaan both sit on Réselia, on either shore of that internal strait — not on two different continents.

**Naming note.** The strait itself doesn't have a settled name beyond "the Almani Corridor," which is specific and already fixed. Whether people also use "the Corridor" loosely for the wider stretch of water around it is still open.

Whoever holds the Corridor holds the choke point of that one continent's internal traffic. Cevine holds it.

## Cevine — the cradle and the hinge

The single most important polity established so far.

**Origin.** The first villages after the Doom sprang up here. Early trade routes formed between them. Those villages consolidated into small kingdoms, and the kingdoms eventually united into Cevine.

**What that means:**

- Cevine is the **oldest continuous civilisation of the new world** — the place where human society restarted. Its prestige is not merely military; it is *primordial*. Other states are upstarts by comparison, and Cevine will never let them forget it.
- Its territory sits on **both shores of the Almani Corridor**, since the villages formed around the trade across it — but both shores are part of **Réselia**. Cevine is a hinge *within* Réselia, not a country suspended between two different continents.
- The **Throne of Thalassus** — the sea-throne — is therefore literal. Cevine's monarchy is founded on water.
- Venice's character with France's mass: a maritime empire of ports, fleets and scattered holdings, resting on a deep territorial core with the manpower to hold it. Its colonies on the third continent follow naturally.
- **It is inherently overextended.** An empire of chokepoints and distant possessions can lose a piece anywhere. Which is why the secession of Parvaan was intolerable — not for the land, but for the precedent.

**Question this raises:** why did the first villages arise *there*? Sheltered from the wave by the geometry of the strait? Elevated? The likeliest answer is that the Corridor's shape protected it, and that is a good answer — luck again, exactly as with the guns.

**Origin of the Throne of Thalassus.** During the Cataclysm, Cevine's founding population sheltered in a cave network within the moving landmass — geologically unusual in staying stable while the terrain around it warped, and fed by a freshwater river running through it. The network was large enough for rudimentary boats to move goods between settlements inside it. At the heart of the river network stood a natural stone formation on a small island, called the Heartstone, used as a landmark to guide the boats.

The cavern was stable relative to the outside world, but not fully stable, and navigating it carried real risk. The Heartstone and the cave-river network itself were public property, but the engineering needed to move goods through it safely — reinforced tunnels, marked channels, lock systems — was not, and the Karois family held the best of that engineering. They charged tolls for its use, and additionally ran a protection service in the mode of the Knights Templar: merchants who contracted Karois escort through the cavern were guaranteed safer passage, and if a merchant died in transit regardless, their goods and trade rights passed to Karois rather than to heirs who had no way to make a claim from the other side of the cavern. Over a hundred years and more, this combination — toll income, engineering monopoly, and the steady one-way transfer of trade rights on death — made Karois wealthy and gave them control of the hub itself well before Thalassus was born. It did not make them rulers of anything wider — they were among the strongest families of the region, not its masters.

**The great houses.** Karois was never the only powerful family of the era — it was the strongest single house, but one great house among several. The others held their own land, wealth, followers, and economic power, and did not depend on Karois the way a poorer family might. Ordinary people had little political power of their own and were not organized against Karois, though the Heartstone's tolls and regulations naturally caused irritation when they obstructed trade.

Thalassus, rightful heir to House Karois, was driven by his own ambition to take part in the Rediscovery, the great movement by which humanity ventured back into the wider world after centuries of isolation. His uncle supported and helped facilitate the expedition — publicly and genuinely — and Thalassus departed Cevine willingly, commanding the ship *Cevine*, spending years at sea enduring extraordinary hardships and encountering peoples, dangers, customs, and forms of power utterly unlike his homeland's.

During his absence, the uncle took the opportunity to take effective control of House Karois and the Heartstone. His rule grew increasingly tyrannical in economic terms — extraordinarily high tolls, burdensome new regulations — causing particular resentment among the great houses whose trade was directly affected. House Karois itself divided: Thalassus's eventual return presented a legitimate hereditary claimant to an office the uncle had simply assumed, and Karois men split between loyalty to the uncle, support for Thalassus, and waiting to see how things fell.

Thalassus came home not as the young heir who had left, but as a legendary figure of the Rediscovery, celebrated for surviving the sea's dangers and for the prestige of what he'd seen and done abroad. He recognized that the uncle's unpopular rule had created an opportunity to turn his own hereditary claim into something far greater than the headship he'd left behind. He secretly secured the support of several great houses, whose men joined his own followers in confronting the uncle at the Heartstone — publicly framed as the restoration of rightful order and relief from tyranny, while privately everyone involved understood the great houses wanted the uncle's intolerable tolls and regulations gone. In that confrontation the uncle was killed, or fell to his death — the precise circumstances are genuinely uncertain in the real record. The later epic tradition supplies the legendary version: that he fell from the Heartstone.

Thalassus then transformed the Karois headship into the Crown of Cevine, proposing himself its first King, and promised the great houses that his uncle's extraordinary tolls would end and the money extracted under them would be returned. The great houses accepted: it removed the uncle, restored their wealth, protected their economic independence, and gave them a political framework — Cevine's own Great Council, formed at this settlement — capable of preventing Karois from ever again using the Heartstone as unchecked leverage. In return, they recognized Thalassus as King. The original Cevinese monarchy was therefore deliberately decentralized from its first day: Karois held the Crown and the Heartstone, but not the power to rule absolutely against the collective strength of the great houses.

The wider population was not consulted in creating the monarchy, but accepted Thalassus readily once it was proclaimed — his survival, his exploits, and his role in the Rediscovery had already made him an extraordinary and widely admired figure before he ever claimed a crown. The great houses built the political settlement; Thalassus's legendary reputation is what made the resulting monarchy acceptable to everyone else.

**A note on naming: Cevine's Great Council is not *the* Great Council.** The Great Council founded at Thalassus's settlement is Cevine's own, standing, domestic body — the great houses collectively, checking the Crown from the kingdom's first day onward. This is a distinct institution from *the* Great Council referenced elsewhere in this world (see `05-states/economy-and-the-council.md`) — the one-time assembly of every ruler in the world, convened once after Queen Renilda's murder to found the Academy, and never reconvened. Same name, different scale, not to be confused.

He extended the river network out to the (by then stabilized) surrounding rivers, letting Cevine apply its existing cave-trade experience to trade with the wider world. The Throne itself was carved into the Heartstone; the palace was built up around it and has since grown to the size of the original cavern, encompassing all of Old Cevine. The original cave and river network survives, physically, at the core of the present-day Royal Palace — but its exact location and extent are known to very few. The ruling House Karois-Marain formed later, when a Queen of Cevine (Thalassus Karois's line) married a King Consort of House Marain, merging the two names. Robert is the first person to bear the combined name — the son of that marriage.

**Fixed geography.** Cevine's heartland is the western shore of the Corridor, and it controls the entire northern stretch of the Corridor on *both* shores — its territory does not stop at the water, it straddles it, which is the literal sense in which Cevine "is" the seam rather than a country next to one.

**Ethnic and linguistic composition.** Cevine is a blend of French, Italian and Spanish descent — the Fenes who migrated south as Europe sank (see `01-cataclysm/the-lost-continent.md`) — layered onto the Arabic-speaking Algerian population that was already on that stretch of coast before the migration. Cevine's culture, language and look should read as that specific mixture, not as generic "Europe."

## Parvaan

Seceded from Cevine ~15 years ago. Was **landlocked**, which is why the **Port of Ouspré** was the one gift that could buy its trust — a great but not major port. Valuable enough to be believed, affordable enough for a naval empire to lose. A ruinous concession would have aroused suspicion; this one didn't.

Ouspré was the only port unfortified against sevaravirus. Roughly a third of Parvaan died.

**Fixed geography.** Parvaan sits on the eastern shore of the Corridor, at the corner where the coastline bends — an L-shaped landmass, with Parvaan occupying the angle of the L. Ouspré is at Parvaan's northernmost point, the tip closest to Cevine's own northern holdings across the water.

## The Marreni Sea of the South — the impact basin

**The Marreni is the crater**, and it lies in the **southern** part of the world, as its name has said all along. The strike that began the Doom landed here, and the sea fills the wound.

*(Generation: the impact is placed at roughly 46° S. Because the pole shift is applied before the strike, that is its final latitude on the finished map.)*

This retro-fits the manuscript exactly. From the text: *"the aquamarine eyes, as deep as the Marreni sea of the South, where sailors would lose their way and poets would find their muses."*

- **"As deep as"** — of course. It is an impact basin. It is the deepest water in the world.
- **"Sailors would lose their way"** — **because their compasses fail over it.** A strike of that magnitude leaves a magnetic anomaly in the rock: the field over the basin is wrong, and has been for fifteen hundred years. Ships lose their heading in open water with a clear sky and no explanation anyone can offer. That line was written before there was a reason for it, and the reason turns out to be the best one available.
- **"Poets would find their muses"** — a sea that swallows navigation and returns nothing accumulates stories. Of course poets go there.

### What this hands you

- **The Doom is legible in the geography,** and nobody has read it. The basin is on every chart. Its ring of islands is charted. The compass failures are a known hazard sailors plan around. Not one person has connected the three.
- **A hazard that is also a hiding place.** Anything that wants not to be found goes where instruments fail.
- **A reader-facing seed of the best kind.** Every element is public, mundane and repeatedly mentioned; the conclusion is never drawn on the page. Readers can assemble it a full book before any character does. (See `07-reference/seeded-mysteries.md`.)
- **A reason the "is the Doom over?" argument stays unresolved.** The one place that could answer it is the one place instruments do not work.

### Open
- Is the ring of islands inhabited? Whoever lives on the rim of the world's deepest water is a strange people with strange customs
- Do the compass failures extend to modern instruments, or only magnetic ones? (Suggestion: modern ones fail differently, which is *more* unnerving)
- Is the sea unusually rich or unusually dead? Both are defensible; dead is eerier
- How far south, and how does it relate to the Corridor?

## Consequences to build on

- **The old world's cities are under water.** Every coast has drowned ruins offshore. Salvage is an industry and a superstition.
- **Some land is new.** Raised seabed carries no ruins, no graves, no ancestral claim. Contested differently from everywhere else.
- **The Corridor is the world's throat.** Every rivalry that matters eventually runs through it, which is your central conflict axis and the reason Cevine is at the centre of the story — even though the Corridor sits inside one continent rather than between two.
