## Paste from here

I am writing **JR** (working title), the first book of a series. It is set on **Earth**, roughly fifteen hundred years after a catastrophe called **the Doom**, which reshaped the planet. I have a partial manuscript and I am building out the world: history, geography, peoples, states and houses. The story is not being written in these documents — they are reference material, so I want **plain, factual, complete prose**: normal sentences and paragraphs, no dramatic fragments, no rhetorical flourishes, no summarising a point and then restating it for effect. Do not editorialize about how something "is remembered/taught/felt" unless I've said so — state facts only.

**Timeline is fixed: 1,500 years total from the Doom to the present**, distributed as: 400y Dark Age (0–400 AC, includes Recovery in its final ~150y) → 300y Rediscovery (400–700 AC: 200y discovery/contact, 100y closing/Elsring) → 400y Colonial Age (700–1100 AC) → 400y Long Acceleration (1100–1500 AC) → present (~1500 AC). Each era is defined by length and starts where the previous ends — change one, the rest cascade. Full detail in `04-history/chronology.md`.

**The Cataclysm** is dated ~2040 in the old (pre-Doom) calendar — close enough to real-world present that AI data centres already existed and were mass-digitising books (destroying originals: cut spine, scan pages, pulp remainder) when it happened. This is why some knowledge was lost despite books generally surviving — some material existed only as server data, and those servers didn't survive. English (not Latin/Greek) is the scholarly bridge language, since English was the dominant academic language circa 2040.

**The premise.** The Doom was not one disaster but a coincidence of several ordinary ones within the same few decades: a geomagnetic reversal already underway, an oceanic impact, the tsunami after it, a decade+ of impact winter, and the failure of already-strained crust. Each was survivable alone; together they were not. **Recorded knowledge survived but the capacity to use it did not** — books need a shelf, factories need ore, fuel, power and trained workforce. The bottleneck was never understanding; it was means. Most old-world technology and objects survived in *some* quantity (a few cars existed through the Dark Age) — what determined survival was continued usefulness, not fragility: cars got dismantled for parts since useless without fuel/roads, guns stayed maintained since useful for hunting/defense (survived in stashes and via working gunsmiths, especially on Lidia). Richer families bought/suppressed some old-world knowledge early on (noble-only); this loosens over later eras but some stays restricted even now. No superstition attaches to intact old-world relics — they're just valuable property.

**Magnetic reversal**, precisely: ordinary periodic kind, long overdue. NOT a physical movement of the planet — axis doesn't shift, no continent changes latitude/climate zone. Real reversals take 2,000–12,000 years, field weakening/wandering throughout, so no reliable magnetic north for centuries. Effects are navigational/biological — useless compasses, thinned magnetosphere, tropical aurorae, collapsed animal migrations. Does NOT cause the climate catastrophe (impact winter does that).

**Europe sank entirely** in the Doom (not just partial coastline loss like everywhere else) and never resurfaced — it's the submerged 7th continent. This started as a map-generation artifact and was made true in-world deliberately. Cevine's founding population (the **Fenes**) are descended from Europeans who fled south as Europe sank, arriving on a coast already home to an Arabic-speaking Algerian population — this is preserved only as garbled family legend (no institutional memory survived the Dark Age at all — no church/university/military order continued functioning), but the legend is substantially true. This is the actual root of Cevine's strong European cultural character, not just geographic proximity. Cevine's specific ethnic/linguistic mix: French + Italian + Spanish (Fenes) layered onto existing Algerian Arabic population.

**Why the world had to be rediscovered.** The impact rearranged the map — land sank, land rose, whole landmasses warped and shifted — and communication between regions failed completely for centuries (total silence early in the Dark Age; rumor/rare contact only near its end). By the time ships were being built again, people sailing out were finding places they didn't know existed — true of Lidia/Réselia finding each other, and of either finding the third continent. Nobody had a working map of anybody, including neighbours. What survives of old geography exists in manuscripts held almost entirely by wealthier families — an accurate account of the old world is private property.

**Geography — decided.** Six continents plus a seventh (submerged, = Europe). **Réselia = the "Africa" continent** (Cevine, Parvaan, the Almani Corridor, the Fenes all here). **Lidia = the "America" continent** (the Academy, and Edward's dukedom — see below). These are two separate, unrelated continents — NOT two halves of one landmass torn apart (an earlier draft claimed this; it was wrong and has been deleted, don't reintroduce it). The **Almani Corridor** is an internal strait within Réselia alone (both its shores are Réselia — Cevine and Parvaan are on the same continent, not two different ones). The third continent (Elsring homeland) is still unnamed. Two more continents are unnamed/small; one polar continent is uninhabited.

**Lidia and Réselia are CONTINENTS, not countries.** Calling someone Lidian/Réselian is like calling someone European. Each contains many sovereign states (100+ direct vassals under Cevine alone).

**Key places.** **Cevine** is the paramount power on Réselia, Venetian-naval-model with France's territorial mass, holding the Corridor's northern stretch on both shores. Its monarchs sit the **Throne of Thalassus** — literal origin: during the Cataclysm, Cevine's founders sheltered in a cave network (unusually stable while surrounding terrain warped) fed by a freshwater river; boats moved goods inside it; a natural stone formation at the river-network's heart, used to guide boats, is where the Throne was later carved. One family won the power struggle for this hub. Generations later, during the Rediscovery, that family's descendant **Thalassus Karois** discovered the Corridor/far shore, returned, and formally founded the Kingdom, taking the throne as first king, extending the river network to the (by-then stabilized) surrounding rivers. The palace has grown to encompass the whole original cavern (= Old Cevine); the cave/river network still physically exists at the Palace's core, location known to very few. House **Karois-Marain** formed later when a Queen of Cevine (Karois line) married a King Consort of House Marain — **Robert is the first person to bear the combined name**, son of that marriage. **Parvaan** seceded from Cevine ~15 years ago (was landlocked), given the **Port of Ouspré** as a calibrated goodwill gesture — valuable enough to be believed, cheap enough to lose — which turned out to be the one port unfortified against sevaravirus; ~1/3 of Parvaan died. Parvaan is the eastern-shore L-shaped landmass at the Corridor's bend, Ouspré at its northernmost point. **Edward's dukedom is the Grand Duchy of Faring, ruling House Faringale, on Lidia.** The **Marreni Sea**, south, is the impact crater; compasses fail there from the magnetic anomaly in the rock (explains an existing manuscript line about sailors losing their way there).

**Technology and society.** Modern-*kind* tech (aircraft, vaccines, cyber-attacks, wristwatches, networks) coexists with feudal political order (kings, dukes, household guards, direct vassals) — but industry never reached or surpassed pre-Cataclysm *scale/production*, staying fractured across many small feudal territories instead of consolidating into one economy. This unevenness (horses beside supercars, windmills beside an airport) is why the world doesn't read as internally contradictory. Political structure never modernized because each tech revolution just made existing power-holders richer rather than displacing them — no bourgeois revolution, the aristocracy bought the factories. ~100–200 sovereign states, layered tiers.

**Dark Age social structure.** No formal serfdom ever developed — just royal family / rich nobles / everyone else who works; early on nobility is fluid (getting rich enough makes you de facto noble), later eras it's rigid and hard to attain. No slavery within Dark Age communities — genuinely taboo, remembered from the old world clearly enough that any noble attempting it would be overthrown by their own people (communities too tight-knit to tolerate it). This taboo does NOT extend to the Elsring during the Rediscovery — conflict began immediately on landing (driven by need for resources), and partial slavery did occur there before mostly dying out or going underground. Village-to-state formation runs in stages: semi-democratic beginnings → informal councils of the respected → entrenchment by founding/rich families → consolidation via absorption/conquest/marriage/diplomacy, with succession calcifying into "sacred" only once memory of how power was first won fades. Houses follow a two-part founding pattern: common base (control of a scarce resource) + one specific "cunning act" per *Great* House (usually uglier than the House's own telling) — timing: early village founders → mid-era consolidation/minor-house fade-out (become non-noble local admin) → Great House founding acts in the era's final quarter, landing right as the Rediscovery begins. Coastal/river-mouth settlements consolidate fastest (Cevine is the textbook example, not an exception). Farming knowledge survives fine (bottleneck is tools/stock, not know-how); livestock mostly collapses but pockets survive (present-day bloodlines descend from these). Language fragments hard into mutual unintelligibility by region (like Vulgar Latin's real breakup) — English survives only as a *written* scholarly bridge; ordinary first contact runs on ad hoc pidgins, elites use written English. Only Europe undertakes a mass migration during this era (forced, since the alternative was drowning) — everyone else stays put out of fear despite worsening conditions. Ground/crust instability is a literal, ongoing hazard through most of the Dark Age and its stabilizing is the actual precondition/trigger for the Dark Age's end (not a fixed calendar date).

**Disease.** **Sevaravirus** is one continuously-evolving viral lineage (not three unrelated diseases stitched together) with multiple historical strains of varying severity: a mild early Dark Age strain, a much worse strain at the Rediscovery's close, and the current present-day strain (the one that hit Ouspré/Parvaan) — same lineage throughout, like real flu/coronavirus strain evolution, not smallpox-then-plague-then-COVID as literally different pathogens.

**The Rediscovery** (400–700 AC). First 200y: discovery/contact phase — Lidia and Réselia find each other first (shorter/calmer crossing than toward the third continent — a *different* stretch of water from the internal Almani Corridor), not by design. Long-distance radio is developed by this era's later years. Last 100y: closing phase — both continents independently (not a planned joint venture) reach and exterminate the Elsring, each fully aware of the other's actions via radio as they happen. **Who actually started the fighting on landing is genuinely unknown/murky** — this ambiguity is why history doesn't treat the Lidian/Réselian response as wholly condemnable, unlike a clean unprovoked conquest. Elsring extermination: disease (largest share) → displacement → firearms (remainder), over ~1 century. Neither Lidia nor Réselia acts as a unified bloc — both are many separate states, several of which fought each other over competing Elsring claims, on top of what both did to the Elsring. The Elsring's own regions found each other late too (frozen/treacherous internal waters) — internally fragmented even longer than Lidia or Réselia were from each other. Colonies funded the next 4 centuries. **Manuscript hard anchor: Elsring exterminated ~800 years before present** (author revised down from an earlier ~1,000 estimate) — lands at the very end of the closing phase (~700 AC), 1500−700=800, exact fit.

Do NOT call this era "the Age of Heroes" — too close to ASOIAF's own era name. Just "the Rediscovery."

**The Elsring.** Native people of the third continent, destroyed as above. Currently almost undeveloped beyond name + fate — open thread if picked up later.

**Do not mention or incorporate any philosophical framework (Laurevinism).** Author has one, introduced later. Build the world on its own terms for now.

**The Academy**, where the book is set, was founded by a near-unanimous Great Council of the world's rulers after the murder of **Queen Renilda**, to raise the heirs of the world together. Sixty students, three classes named for colours, on Lidia. The Council met once and has never met again. **Deliberately deferred to later** — don't invent who killed Renilda or who proposed the Academy; author will address this "way into the future."

**Maps.** Finalized/frozen base map exists in `maps/` (`WORLD_sundered_shifted_elevation.npy` + renders) — built via independent per-continent rotation/placement of real Earth landmasses, `sunder_pair` used to split Africa into the Corridor's two shores, Marreni impact crater carved into a natural bay on the American-supercontinent coastline. Azgaar's Fantasy Map Generator integration (heightmap import, Playwright automation) is built and working but **explicitly deferred** ("we'll do azgaar and the rest later"). One pending map edit: move the Elsring continent further north to increase distance from Lidia/Réselia (reinforces why it was found later/separately).

**Worldbuilding process/approach:** top-down like GRRM/ASOIAF — broad world history and load-bearing facts first, political/state-level minutiae later (author judged Cevine/Parvaan detail was tackled prematurely earlier in the process). In practice this session's actual method has been closer to a GRRM/Sanderson mix: reverse-engineering canon from what the manuscript already implies, then pushing into detail only on load-bearing pieces (Sanderson's "pick a few things, go deep"), rather than uniform top-down design from scratch.

Please continue helping me build this world. Ask me questions where decisions are needed (quiz/multiple-choice format is the established working pattern) rather than inventing answers, and keep the register factual — plain prose, no drama, no editorializing about how events "are remembered/felt" unless I've said so.
